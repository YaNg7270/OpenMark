# Calculus Displays

Limits and derivatives are the core vocabulary of single-variable calculus,
and they read best as displayed equations that stand apart from the prose
which introduces them.

The limit of a rational function as the variable grows without bound is the
first example.

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x} = 0
$$

A first derivative is written with the differential operator, which is the
conventional way to state a rate of change.

$$
\frac{d}{dx} x^3 = 3x^2
$$

A partial derivative drops the redundant variable and keeps the one that
actually varies.

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
$$

A definite integral over a finite interval carries both limits on the
integral sign itself.

$$
\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx
$$

A double integral extends the same idea to a plane region.

$$
\iint_{D} f(x, y) \, dA
$$

A contour integral runs around a closed curve and appears whenever complex
analysis is on the agenda.

$$
\oint_{C} f(z) \, dz
$$

Six displays appear in this file, covering a limit, two kinds of derivative,
and three kinds of integral.
